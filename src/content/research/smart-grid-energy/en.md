The NCUE campus runs a 1-MW battery energy storage system (BESS) alongside rooftop solar. The lab applies AI and edge computing to its operation: edge devices process the measurements with the discrete wavelet transform (DWT), the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) and Huffman coding, and LSTM neural networks approximate and predict how the system behaves. <!-- source: Prof. Yang's TEEP 2023 (SDG 7) and IIPP 2024 slides, provided by Brian on 2026-10-18 -->

The work follows the whole chain: the battery modules (state of charge, balancing, C-rate), the power conversion system (charging and discharging, fault detection, voltage, current, harmonics and power factor), power-system dynamics and frequency, and power quality at the point of common coupling. The aim is a zero-energy campus, in line with UN Sustainable Development Goal 7 (affordable and clean energy). The lab also measures and models supercapacitors and batteries. <!-- source: Brian, 2026-10-17 (1-F supercapacitor data) -->

The direction builds on Prof. Yang's earlier work in power electronics and power quality, listed below: wavelet-based voltage-sag monitoring, flicker measurement, power-disturbance recognition with neural networks, flywheel energy storage, battery charging and resonant converters.
